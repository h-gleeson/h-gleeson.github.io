Palette studies

Ten directions for replacing the free accent picker with a fixed, designed color system: one dark mode and one light mode per palette, each built on a named color principle. The newest are at the top.

Every preview uses the real homepage card styles. The numbers are WCAG contrast ratios, worked out live from the colors: 4.5:1 is the AA minimum for body text, 3:1 for large text and interface parts, 7:1 is AAA. Nothing here changes the site.

How these were built

  • 60-30-10. Roughly 60% background, 30% text and surfaces, 10% accent. The accent marks what's interactive or important, so it stays meaningful.
  • Tinted neutrals. No pure greys (except the Swiss study, where that's the point). Each palette's backgrounds and text lean slightly toward its accent or its complement, so the accent looks like it belongs.
  • Two accent tones per mode. A fill tone for buttons and highlights, and a text tone adjusted until it reads at 4.5:1 or better on the page and panel colors.
  • Dark isn't inverted light. Dark backgrounds sit around 5–8% lightness rather than pure black, text is off-white to avoid glare, and accents are lifted and slightly desaturated so they don't vibrate.
  • Cards belong to the page. The card frame, background, title banner and text all come from the palette: drawn in the accent, the heading ink, a neutral, or (where the principle calls for one) a support material like chipboard or sage. In light mode the cards turn light too.
  • Grounded references. The newer palettes start from real materials and processes (trace paper, diazo prints, model board) rather than from the color wheel, which keeps them from looking too perfectly balanced.
